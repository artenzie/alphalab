## Why these choices

- **Sizing at the decision close, filling at the next open.** The agent's numbers decide the shares, so an unchanged weight never trades. The cost is that a fully invested agent can go a little below zero cash, which pays margin interest like any other borrowing.
- **"Agent 1 = SPY minus one slippage charge" is only approximately true.** The exact statement includes the overnight gap: return = (O/C₀)·[(P_end/O − 1) − 0.0005] − interest/V₀. The script checks this to the cent rather than the loose version.
- **Accruals in arrears.** Each calendar day is charged at the next close, on the balances of the last close (Monday's close charges Fri, Sat and Sun on Friday's book). This needs no future calendar, and nothing is charged before it happens. The run's last close is never charged for the days after it, which is a few cents at most.
- **Margin vs decision.** The margin check always runs and is logged. A non-hold decision replaces the forced cut, because risk-checked targets (gross ≤ 150%) already keep equity above 30% of gross.
- **Splits stop the ledger.** In live mode, a split between the last close and the fill raises `SplitDetected` for a manual correction, instead of trading pre-split shares at post-split prices. Backtest mode uses adjusted prices throughout, so it needs no guard.
- **Crypto deferred.** "Next price after 00:15Z" needs intraday bars we don't store. The core already does the crypto fee maths; the DB layer refuses crypto until that source is built.
- **Accounting identity as the main test.** Over 500 random days with shorts, leverage, weekends and fees, Δequity = price P&L − slippage − fees − borrow − interest to the cent. If the ledger ever leaks or invents money, this test fails.

## Later: cash floor (protocol v1.6)

The first smoke run showed agent 2's day-1 buys taking cash to −$69.51, so a long-only, 100%-gross agent was paying margin interest ($0.70). That's borrowing its limits say it can't do.

**New rule 12.** Agents 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 14, C0 and C1 fill sells first. If the buys would then take cash below 0, all that day's buys are scaled down pro rata, so cash ends at 0.
- Each scaled buy is logged as a `cash_floor` risk event.
- The fill row holds the scaled qty and the event id; the order row stays as placed.
- Agents that short (4, 6, 8–13) or may exceed 100% gross (15) are unchanged and still pay interest on negative cash.
- The list comes from `risk.PROFILES` (no shorts, max gross 100%), not a hard-coded set.

**Notes:**
- **Why "about 0".** Scaled shares are rounded down to 9 decimals, and the scale factor carries a 1e-12 haircut, so cash ends just above 0 (under $0.000001), never below.
- **Dust buys.** A buy with only such crumbs left to fund it fills a few billionths of a share instead of being cancelled. Only cash ≤ 0 exactly cancels a buy.
- **Small buys still fill.** A scaled buy is filled even under $100, because the minimum is a decision-time rule.
- **Return drops a little.** Agent 2 now holds slightly fewer shares, which costs 0.16 bp over this rising window.
